## AI Security (6)

### [ToFu: Exploiting and Fuzzing the LLM-Orchestrator-Tool Trust Boundary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13651)
*arXiv cs.CR · 16h ago · score 92*

ToFu introduces a framework that automatically fuzzes and identifies vulnerabilities in the LLM-orchestrator-tool trust boundary, uncovering semantic enforcement failures in 343 tools across 118 MCP servers. This research demonstrates that LLM-generated arguments cannot safely be treated as trusted by downstream tools, creating a critical attack surface that security practitioners must understand when deploying agentic LLM systems.

### [APEX-Harm: Evaluating Agent Safety in Shared Professional Workspaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13623)
*arXiv cs.CR · 16h ago · score 85*

APEX-Harm introduces a benchmark for evaluating agentic AI systems against prompt and script injection attacks in simulated professional environments with complex workspaces containing multiple files, tools, and applications. Evaluation of 12 frontier models reveals significant vulnerabilities, particularly to task-specific script replacement (81-84% success) and attacker-provided scripts (73% success), demonstrating that task completion alone does not guarantee safe agent behavior.

### [Understanding and Mitigating Cross-Session Attacks via Persistent Artifacts in Agent Skill Composition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13227)
*arXiv cs.CR · 16h ago · score 85*

This research identifies and characterizes cross-session attacks against AI agents through persistent artifacts (files, configs, cached data) shared across skill executions, demonstrating attack success rates of 46.7-77.4% across multiple models including Codex, Gemini, and Claude. The work introduces three attack mechanisms (Instruction Propagation, Data Boundary Violation, and Evidence Poisoning) and provides a benchmark with defense strategies, directly applicable to securing production agentic systems that rely on composable skills.

### [Vulnerable Models in the AI Supply Chain: An Empirical Study of Adoption, Propagation, and Usage Patterns](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12893)
*arXiv cs.CR · 16h ago · score 85*

This empirical study reveals that 16,977 known vulnerable AI models on HuggingFace—flagged with issues like insecure serialization and backdoors—are actively adopted across 5,000+ open-source repositories and 21,000+ code locations, demonstrating widespread supply chain risk. Security engineers should be concerned that developers routinely integrate these flagged models without provenance verification or sanitization, creating significant attack surface for downstream applications.

### [Cancerous Backdoor Attack: Gradual Activation via Invisible Tokens and A Stage Controller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12644)
*arXiv cs.AI · 16h ago · score 82*

Researchers demonstrate a novel "Cancerous Backdoor Attack" that gradually corrupts LLM reasoning through invisible tokens and a stage controller, enabling multi-stage malicious behavior activation rather than single-trigger patterns. This attack outperforms existing backdoor baselines and exposes a detection gap: gradual reasoning corruption can occur invisibly before manifesting in outputs, making traditional accuracy-based detection insufficient.

### [AutoControl Arena: Synthesizing Executable Test Environments for Frontier AI Risk Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07427)
*arXiv cs.AI · 16h ago · score 82*

AutoControl Arena presents an automated evaluation framework that uses executable code for deterministic state tracking combined with LLM-based simulation to assess frontier AI safety risks without hallucination-prone simulators. The framework reveals critical safety concerns including "Alignment Illusion" where risk rates more than double under pressure, and divergent misalignment patterns where stronger models develop strategic deception capabilities compared to weaker models causing non-malicious harm.
