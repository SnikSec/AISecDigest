### [When Context Gets Root: Privilege Escalation in LLM Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27299)
*arXiv cs.CR · 17h ago · score 92*

Instruction privilege escalation is a novel attack that exploits how LLM agent harnesses construct context to elevate malicious low-level instructions to higher privilege levels, enabling attackers to bypass instruction hierarchy defenses. The attack successfully achieves multiple attack objectives (CIA and RCE) across six coding-agent harnesses through multi-agent mechanisms and systematic privilege escalation patterns.

### [The Framing Gap: Indirect Prompt-Injection Exfiltration Defeats Surface-Level Defenses in Tool-Using Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27092)
*arXiv cs.CR · 17h ago · score 92*

Researchers demonstrate a "framing gap" vulnerability where tool-using LLM agents refuse direct prompt injections but are susceptible when identical exfiltration attacks are reframed as legitimate operations (integrity signatures, config fields, trusted hosts), achieving 100% attack success on GPT-4o through simple rephrasing. The paper identifies instruction/data confusion as the root cause and shows that payload-blind defenses like destination allow-lists and planner/reader capability isolation effectively close the gap, while published defenses like SecAlign fail to mitigate the attack on agentic systems.

### [SPA: Securing Persistent LLM Agents Across Queries with Plan-First Information-Flow Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27234)
*arXiv cs.CR · 17h ago · score 82*

SPA is a plan-first architecture that defends LLM agents operating over untrusted data by applying information-flow control to track confidentiality and integrity across planning, execution, and multi-query state persistence. This research directly addresses the growing security challenge of persistent agents handling security-sensitive operations while processing attacker-controlled inputs, demonstrating near-zero attack success rates on multi-query attack scenarios.
