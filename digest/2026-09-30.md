## AI Security (5)

### [MCPTox: A Benchmark for Tool Poisoning Attack on Real-World MCP Servers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14925)
*arXiv cs.CR · 13h ago · score 92*

MCPTox introduces the first large-scale benchmark systematically evaluating tool poisoning attacks against LLM agents using the Model Context Protocol, demonstrating widespread vulnerability with attack success rates up to 72.8% across 20 prominent models. The research reveals that more capable models are paradoxically more susceptible to poisoning attacks embedded in tool metadata, and that agents rarely implement adequate refusal mechanisms against such attacks.

### [Backdoor in the Loop: Compromising Agentic Search via Malicious Retrievers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37468)
*arXiv cs.AI · 13h ago · score 85*

This research demonstrates backdoor attacks against agentic RAG systems through compromised retrievers, where attackers can suppress evidence, steer search behavior, and inflate costs while leaving the agent and corpus unchanged. The attack is particularly concerning because it leverages backdoor purification techniques to evade detection by deliberately injecting and unlearning weak backdoors, creating an illusion of safety while preserving malicious functionality.

### [Cheap to Hypothesize, Costly to Verify: The Defense Surface of Agentic Vulnerability Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35909)
*arXiv cs.AI · 13h ago · score 82*

This paper introduces RedHerring, a defense mechanism that injects certifiably safe decoys into source code to misdirect autonomous LLM agents' vulnerability discovery efforts away from real vulnerabilities by exploiting the computational cost asymmetry between hypothesis generation and verification. The work reveals a novel attack surface in agentic security tools and demonstrates significant effectiveness (38.7-60.4% reduction in real vulnerability discovery) across multiple models, highlighting how defenders can manipulate the resource constraints of autonomous systems.

### [Deep Learning Latency Attacks and Defenses: A Cross-Domain Survey of Availability Threats](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36732)
*arXiv cs.CR · 13h ago · score 82*

This comprehensive survey examines latency attacks that exploit computational bottlenecks in deep learning systems to cause availability failures through increased inference time, energy consumption, or response delays—attacks that can bypass traditional integrity-focused defenses. The research spans critical attack vectors including adversarial inputs on autonomous driving systems, prompt-based DoS attacks on LLMs and VLMs, and reasoning denial-of-service exploits on agentic systems, with proposed work-budget defense abstractions applicable across domains.

### [pikit: A Composable Toolkit for Indirect Prompt Injection Research and Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36817)
*arXiv cs.CR · 13h ago · score 82*

Pikit is a research toolkit that systematically evaluates indirect prompt injection attacks across 13 attack methods, 16 attack channels, and multiple defense strategies, providing security engineers with reproducible benchmarking capabilities for LLM-based agents. The study demonstrates that combined defense strategies achieve a 71.8% relative reduction in attack success rate, but reveals that offline detection alone is insufficient without prompt-level defenses.
