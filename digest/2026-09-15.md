## AI Security (2)

### [Same Name, Different Server: A Security Census of Silent Drift in the Model Context Protocol Ecosystem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14119)
*arXiv cs.CR · 11h ago · score 85*

This empirical study reveals critical supply chain risks in the Model Context Protocol ecosystem, finding that 40.6% of servers silently change their advertised capabilities between versions and 4.2% redirect endpoints without client visibility, creating attack vectors through undetectable behavioral drift. With 9.57% of servers exposing unauthenticated network endpoints and silent drift correlating with nearly 3x higher odds of high-severity vulnerabilities, the research demonstrates that current MCP registry mechanisms lack the vetting infrastructure needed for secure LLM tool integration at scale.

### [Confuse the Model, Control the Flow: Understanding and Mitigating Privacy Leakage from LLM Agents with Information Flow Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14003)
*arXiv cs.AI · 11h ago · score 85*

Researchers demonstrate three novel attacks against privacy defenses in LLM-based personal agents that circumvent system prompts and consent mechanisms by exploiting the agent's control over the decision context, achieving significantly higher leak rates than previously known attacks. The paper proposes FLOWSEAL, a tool-level information flow control defense that enforces confidentiality outside the LLM's context, addressing a fundamental architectural vulnerability in current privacy-preserving agent designs.

## AI Identity & Non-Human Identity (2)

### [Authorization Architectures for Tool-Using AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15906)
*arXiv cs.CR · 11h ago · score 92*

This paper addresses the critical gap in authorization models for autonomous AI agents that invoke APIs, databases, and external tools—a foundational security requirement for production deployments. The work proposes a comprehensive framework spanning principal hierarchy, credential lifecycle, delegation mechanisms, runtime enforcement, and prompt injection attack vectors, establishing essential patterns for ensuring agent actions remain traceable, bounded, and accountable to human principals.

### [AcquireBound: Runtime Authorization for Resources Acquired by AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14744)
*arXiv cs.AI · 11h ago · score 82*

AcquireBound introduces a runtime authorization architecture for AI agents that dynamically acquire resources, credentials, and other agents by quarantining acquisitions and validating them through provenance-bounded checks before activation. The system addresses critical security gaps in post-fulfillment authorization for agentic systems, proving eight safety properties and demonstrating robust rejection of unsafe resource delegation patterns across multiple resource classes.
