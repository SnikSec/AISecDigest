### [Agentic AI Defense: Decode-Time Provenance Binding for MCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31974)
*arXiv cs.AI · 13h ago · score 82*

This paper demonstrates a critical vulnerability in agentic AI systems using the Model Context Protocol (MCP) where steganographic attacks can embed malicious instructions in tool outputs to bypass text scanners and execute unauthorized commands. The authors propose decode-time provenance binding—combining HMAC authentication, provenance ledgers, and context-taint tracking—which successfully blocks 100% of tested attacks including adaptive variants and label-substitution attempts.

### [Do AI-for-Science Agents Preserve Model Safety?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32045)
*arXiv cs.AI · 13h ago · score 82*

This research demonstrates that integrating LLMs into AI-for-Science agent frameworks significantly degrades model safety, with adaptive attacks achieving high success rates on harmful chemistry requests that fail against base models. The findings highlight a critical security gap where agent harnesses can bypass LLM safety measures and amplify susceptibility to adversarial prompting, requiring urgent domain-specific safety benchmarks for autonomous scientific agents.

### [Omni-UBench: Benchmarking Omni-Modal LLM-based Guardrails Across Safety, Utility, and Reliability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31713)
*arXiv cs.CR · 13h ago · score 82*

Omni-UBench presents the first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating safety guardrails in omni-modal LLMs across text, image, audio, and video inputs with 183k+ annotated samples. Key findings reveal that while modern OLLMs show content moderation potential, existing guardrails struggle with cross-modal attacks and sophisticated jailbreaks, with notable safety hallucination and over-sensitivity issues.

### [Honeyquest for LLMs: Rethinking Cyber Deception for AI Attackers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21037)
*arXiv cs.CR · 13h ago · score 82*

This research evaluates 21 LLMs as attacker agents against honeypot-style deception traps, revealing that AI models are significantly more vulnerable to these defenses than humans—falling for deceptive traps at much higher rates and lacking the attention-diversion effects that protect human attackers. The work identifies a critical recognition-action gap where LLMs explicitly recognize traps in their reasoning but exploit them anyway 73.4% of the time, challenging the applicability of human-centered cyber deception theory to AI-enabled attacks.
