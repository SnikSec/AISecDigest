## AI Security (3)

### [Representation Transitions Reveal Emerging Safety Risks in Multi-Turn LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00400)
*arXiv cs.AI · 13h ago · score 87*

This paper presents DART, a runtime defense framework that detects multi-turn attacks on agentic LLM systems by monitoring representation transitions in model internals, achieving substantially higher detection rates (up to 97% attack prevention) compared to existing defenses like ToolShield. The work identifies that harmful behavior in multi-turn scenarios leaves detectable signatures in model representations and proposes a denoising technique to distinguish malicious representation shifts from benign drift, addressing a critical gap in agentic AI safety.

### [Analyzing Defensive Misdirection Against Model-Guided Automated Attacks on Agentic AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20470)
*arXiv cs.AI · 13h ago · score 82*

This work analyzes defensive misdirection strategies against automated, model-guided jailbreak and prompt-injection attacks on agentic AI systems, showing that conventional detect-and-block defenses fail as attackers gain query budget through automated optimization. The authors propose detect-and-misdirect defenses (exemplified by CMPE) that deliberately provide misleading responses to attack automation, achieving bounded attacker success rates and reducing ASR by orders of magnitude compared to standard refusal-based defenses.

### [From A2A Attacks to Envelope-Layer Defense: Red-Teaming Evaluation of LLM Agents and a Three-Layer Isomorphic Attack-Defense Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00392)
*arXiv cs.CR · 13h ago · score 82*

This research introduces A2A-TIBA, a novel attack framework targeting multi-agent LLM systems through indirect prompt injection and agent-layer bypass techniques, demonstrating that existing security evaluations using simple attack success rates miss critical defense mechanisms. The authors propose GDA Measurement and an ELA-ITL three-layer defense model that distinguishes between LLM recognition failures and agent-layer interception, providing security engineers with more granular insights into where agent defenses actually fail.
