## AI Security (5)

### [Do Agents Dream of False Memories? Black-box Visual Attacks on Long-term Memory in Multimodal AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15657)
*arXiv cs.CR · 9h ago · score 92*

This research demonstrates black-box adversarial attacks on multimodal AI agents' long-term memory systems, enabling attackers to inject imperceptible perturbations into images to poison or inject false memories. The attack requires no access to the target model itself and achieves high success rates across multiple commercial memory architectures, representing a critical vulnerability in production systems that rely on persistent visual context.

### [Latent Fusion Jailbreak: Blending Harmful and Harmless Representations to Elicit Unsafe LLM Outputs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10029)
*arXiv cs.AI · 9h ago · score 92*

Latent Fusion Jailbreak (LFJ) demonstrates a highly effective white-box attack achieving 94.13% success rate by interpolating hidden states between harmful and benign queries at strategic layers, highlighting critical vulnerabilities in safety-aligned LLMs beyond prompt-level defenses. The research includes a latent adversarial training defense reducing ASR to 12.37%, providing security engineers with both attack mechanisms and preliminary mitigation strategies to understand and protect against internal representation manipulation.

### [AgentRedBench: Dynamic Redteaming and Integration-Aware Defense for LLM Agents over SaaS Integrations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02240)
*arXiv cs.AI · 9h ago · score 92*

AgentRedBench introduces a dynamic redteaming benchmark demonstrating that indirect prompt injection attacks on LLM agents integrating with SaaS services succeed 32-81% of the time across leading models, with a particular focus on underspecified-authorization scenarios across 24 enterprise integrations. The work releases AgentRedGuard, an open-source defense achieving 75-77 percentage point attack success reduction while maintaining 0% false-positive rate on benign interactions, addressing a concrete production threat currently under-measured by existing benchmarks.

### [Code-Poisoning Property Inference Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15970)
*arXiv cs.CR · 9h ago · score 85*

Code-Poisoning Property Inference Attacks (CPPIA) introduce a novel code-level privacy attack where adversaries embed malicious code into public repositories to extract global properties of private training data when developers unknowingly train models on poisoned code. This attack achieves 100% accuracy without degrading model performance and bypasses existing defenses, representing a critical supply-chain threat for ML development workflows relying on GitHub and AI coding agents like Codex.

### [AI Watermark Evidence Fails Forensic Readiness: An Empirical Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16010)
*arXiv cs.CR · 9h ago · score 85*

This empirical evaluation reveals critical failures in LLM watermarking methods (KGW, Unigram, SynthID) when tested against forensic standards and legal admissibility criteria, with paraphrase attacks achieving 98-100% watermark removal rates and baseline false-negative rates of 70-83%. The findings directly challenge regulatory assumptions in the EU AI Act and California's SB 942, which mandate watermarks for AI-generated content as evidence-grade authentication mechanisms.
